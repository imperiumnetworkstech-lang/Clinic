## 3. What's weak

**Duplicate and borrowed content (SEO risk).** The 15 FAQs and the 3 blog posts read as generic web copy, not the clinic's own writing. There are US references ("costing much more in dollars", "American Dental Association"), and one find-and-replace artefact: "Chewing **deskulates** the flow of your saliva", which should read "stimulates". Text like this almost certainly exists word for word elsewhere, which gains no rankings and weakens E-E-A-T.
→ **Recommendation:** migrate them as drafts flagged `needs-doctor-review`, then rewrite them in the clinic's own voice with a named reviewing doctor. They will not be published verbatim.

**Medical accuracy needs review.** The "Oral Cancer" post cites survival percentages with no source, and its featured image is a graphic clinical photo of a tongue lesion. It needs a doctor review, references, and a less distressing image.

**Treatments are one-liners.** Each needs a dedicated page of 600–1,200 words. I will draft original explanatory copy marked `needs-doctor-review`; clinic-specific facts (sittings, technique, materials) stay as placeholders.

**Images**

- Alt text is "portfolio", "logo" or empty everywhere.
- Photos are 800–1350px JPEGs of 600–870 KB, with no WebP or AVIF versions. They are good, natural photos of the doctor and team, which is a real asset.
- Patients are identifiable in 6 photos (52, 53, 55, 56, 57, and 39/49/51 partially). These need consent confirmation before reuse.
- The blog images are 320px clip-art.

**Copy quality.** Typos and template text: "Gallary", "patient’s feel", "Advance Treatments", "Assissted", "theesthetics", and "Alexendra Faraday … Dromley Kent" (probably Bromley; we keep testimonials exactly as published unless the client corrects them).

**Testimonials** have no dates and no source. Every one shows 5 template stars, and the slider repeats them 3 times. Separately, the Google Business Profile (Mapusa) shows **4.6★ from 59 reviews** (read 2026-11-03). That is real, current social proof, but we can only show it as a linked badge, never as self-serving `AggregateRating` schema.

**Technical SEO is effectively absent:** identical titles and descriptions everywhere, no `h1` hierarchy, no schema, no sitemap, no robots.txt, numeric URLs (`/service/view/19`), a dead `/single-blog.html` link, a `mailto:info@youwebsite.com` template link, and ShareThis widgets plus a Facebook SDK loaded on every page. The "Book Appointment" button just dials the phone.

## 5. Insights that change the plan

1. **International / dental-tourism patients are a real segment.** Three of the four website testimonials are from UK patients (Essex, London, Kent), and a Google review from a UK patient describes long-term patients returning "year on year". Candolim is a tourist beach belt, and nearby competitors (e.g. Charmin' Smile, Marigold, Goa Dental Clinic) market directly to overseas patients. → I've added a dental-tourist persona, an "International patients" page, and UK-intent keywords.
2. **The dental microscope** (photos 39, 49, 51) supports the "Root canal & cosmetic specialist" positioning. It's the best "technology" story the clinic has, and the 3D section should tie into it.
3. The **specialists board** in the shop-front photo lists 6 doctors (Dr. Sandeep, Dr. Santosh, Dr. Deepa, Dr. Sravani, Dr. Shraddha Nagve…, Dr. Neil). It suggests a multi-specialty team, including a pedodontist and an implantologist, but we will publish none of them until the client confirms.

## 6. Placeholder convention

- `[[CLIENT TO PROVIDE: …]]`: the fact is missing.
- `[[CLIENT TO CONFIRM: …]]`: we have a candidate value that needs verification.
- `needs-doctor-review`: new or migrated medical copy that a dentist must approve before publishing.

The build will refuse to publish (CI check) any page that still contains `[[CLIENT TO`.
